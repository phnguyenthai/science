# What problem did Nikola Tesla help solve?

## The short answer

Nikola Tesla helped solve a practical problem: **how to generate and deliver electricity over useful distances, and how to turn that electricity into motion for machines.**

His important contributions included a polyphase alternating-current (AC) system and the AC induction motor. These ideas became important parts of the electrical systems developed and built by many engineers and companies.

## Why was this a problem?

In the late 1800s, electric lights and machines were becoming useful, but electricity still had to be generated and delivered to where people needed it.

Early direct-current (DC) systems worked, but at the voltages then commonly used they were less practical for sending large amounts of power over long distances: electrical resistance in the wires wasted energy as heat. Generating stations therefore had to be relatively close to many customers.

Alternating current changes direction repeatedly. With transformers, AC voltage can be raised for transmission and lowered again for safer use. For the same amount of power, transmitting at a higher voltage allows a lower current, which reduces resistive losses in the wires.

## What did Tesla contribute?

Tesla developed and patented a polyphase AC system, including designs for motors that could run from alternating current. His induction motor used a rotating magnetic field to make a rotor turn, providing a practical way to power machinery without brushes or a direct electrical connection to the rotor.

Tesla's AC patents were licensed to George Westinghouse's company. Westinghouse and many other workers and organizations helped develop, manufacture, and build AC equipment and power systems. Tesla's work was significant, but it was part of a larger engineering effort.

## What changed?

AC systems made it practical to transmit electricity at high voltage, transform it for different stages of delivery, and use it to run motors. This helped electrical power reach beyond the immediate area around a generating station and supported the growth of electric lighting and industry.

## What Tesla did not do

- Tesla did not discover electricity or invent every part of the electrical grid.
- He was not the only person who developed AC power; engineers including Hippolyte Pixii, Lucien Gaulard, John Dixon Gibbs, William Stanley Jr., Galileo Ferraris, and others contributed to the field.
- His work did not make electricity lossless or make every electrical problem disappear.

## In one sentence

**Tesla helped make polyphase AC power and practical AC motors part of a system that could deliver electricity over distance and use it to do useful work.**
